# Appendix - Glossary of Terms

This appendix provides definitions for key terms used throughout the AI-Humanoid-Robotics Book.

## A

**Actuator**: A component of a robot that converts energy into physical movement, such as a motor or servo.

**AI (Artificial Intelligence)**: The simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems.

**Angular Momentum**: The rotational equivalent of linear momentum, particularly important in humanoid balance control.

**APC (Advanced Personal Computer)**: A high-performance computing platform for robotics applications.

**Autonomous**: Capable of operating independently without human intervention.

## B

**Balance Control**: The ability of a robot to maintain its center of mass within its support polygon.

**Bipedal**: Having two legs, referring to walking on two legs like humans.

**Bipedal Locomotion**: The act of walking on two legs, a key capability for humanoid robots.

**Behavior Tree**: A hierarchical structure used in robotics and AI for organizing and executing behaviors.

**Biomechanics**: The study of the structure, function, and motion of the mechanical aspects of biological systems.

## C

**Capture Point**: A point on the ground where a biped can step to stop its current motion and stand still.

**Center of Mass (CoM)**: The point in a body where the total mass of the body is considered to be concentrated.

**Cognitive Planning**: The process of creating a sequence of actions to achieve a goal, involving reasoning and decision making.

**Computer Vision**: A field of artificial intelligence that trains computers to interpret and understand the visual world.

**Control Theory**: An engineering and mathematics field dealing with the behavior of dynamical systems with inputs.

## D

**Deep Learning**: A subset of machine learning that uses neural networks with multiple layers to model complex patterns.

**Degrees of Freedom (DOF)**: The number of independent movements a mechanical system can make.

**Dexterous Manipulation**: The ability to perform precise and skillful manipulation tasks with robot hands.

**Digital Twin**: A virtual representation of a physical object or system that spans its lifecycle.

**Dynamics**: The branch of mechanics concerned with the motion of bodies under the action of forces.

## E

**Embodied AI**: Artificial intelligence that is integrated into a physical form that can interact with the real world.

**Embodied Intelligence**: Intelligence that emerges from the interaction between an agent and its environment.

**End-Effector**: The device at the end of a robotic arm, designed to interact with the environment.

**Episodic Memory**: A category of long-term memory that involves the recollection of specific events.

## F

**Forward Kinematics**: The use of joint parameters to compute the position and orientation of the end-effector.

**Force Control**: A control strategy that regulates the forces exerted by a robot on its environment.

**Force/Torque Sensor**: A sensor that measures the forces and torques applied to a robot's end-effector or joints.

## G

**Gazebo**: A 3D simulation environment for robotics that provides accurate physics simulation.

**Geometric Jacobian**: A matrix that relates the joint velocities to the end-effector velocity.

**Grasp Planning**: The process of determining how to grasp an object with a robot hand.

**Gripper**: A device that grasps an object, typically at the end of a robot arm.

## H

**Human-Robot Interaction (HRI)**: The study of interactions between humans and robots.

**Humanoid**: Having human-like characteristics, especially in form or behavior.

**Humanoid Robotics**: A branch of robotics focused on creating robots with human-like characteristics.

## I

**IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit)**: A device that measures and reports a body's specific force, angular rate, and sometimes magnetic field.

**Inverse Dynamics**: The calculation of forces and torques required to achieve a desired motion.

**Inverse Kinematics**: The mathematical process of calculating joint parameters from end-effector position.

**Isaac Sim**: NVIDIA's reference simulation application and synthetic data generation tool for robotics.

**Isaac ROS**: Hardware-accelerated perception and navigation packages that bridge robotics and AI.

## J

**Jacobian**: A matrix of partial derivatives that describes the relationship between joint velocities and end-effector velocities.

**Joint**: A connection between two or more links in a robotic system that allows relative motion.

## K

**Kinematics**: The branch of mechanics concerned with the motion of objects without reference to the forces that cause the motion.

**Kinesthetic Perception**: The perception of body position, weight, or movement through sensory mechanisms.

## L

**Large Language Model (LLM)**: A language model that contains many parameters and is trained on large amounts of text data.

**Legged Locomotion**: The ability of a robot to move using legs, particularly important for humanoid robots.

**LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)**: A remote sensing method that uses light in the form of a pulsed laser to measure distances.

**Locomotion**: The ability to move from one place to another, particularly relevant for mobile robots.

## M

**Manipulation**: The ability of a robot to handle and control objects in its environment.

**Manipulator**: A robot arm designed to manipulate objects in the environment.

**Machine Learning**: A type of artificial intelligence that enables computers to learn and make decisions from data.

**Multi-Modal Perception**: The integration of information from multiple sensory modalities for better understanding.

## N

**Navigation**: The ability of a robot to move through an environment to reach a goal location.

**Natural Language Processing (NLP)**: A field of AI focused on the interaction between computers and humans using natural language.

**Neural Network**: A computing system inspired by the human brain, used in machine learning.

**Node**: In ROS, a process that performs computation, communicating with other nodes through messages.

## O

**Operational Space Control**: A control framework that allows control of a robot's end-effector in Cartesian space.

**Omniverse**: NVIDIA's scalable, multi-GPU real-time simulation and design collaboration platform.

## P

**Perception**: The ability of a robot to sense and interpret its environment.

**Physical AI**: AI systems that interact with and operate in the physical world.

**PID Controller**: A control loop mechanism employing proportional, integral, and derivative terms.

**Point Cloud**: A set of data points in space, typically representing the external surface of an object.

**Pose**: The position and orientation of an object in space.

## R

**Reactive Planning**: A planning approach that responds to changes in the environment in real-time.

**Reinforcement Learning**: A type of machine learning where an agent learns to make decisions by performing actions and receiving rewards.

**ROS (Robot Operating System)**: A flexible framework for writing robot software.

**ROS 2**: The second generation of the Robot Operating System with improved features.

**RSL (Robotics Software Lab)**: A laboratory environment for robotics software development.

**Rviz**: The 3D visualization tool for ROS.

## S

**Sensor Fusion**: The process of combining data from multiple sensors to improve understanding of the environment.

**SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping)**: The computational problem of constructing or updating a map of an unknown environment.

**Social Robotics**: The study of robots that interact with humans in a socially acceptable manner.

**Stereovision**: The perception of depth and 3D structure obtained from binocular vision.

**System Integration**: The process of bringing together component sub-systems into one system.

## T

**Task Planning**: The process of creating a sequence of actions to achieve a specific goal.

**Trajectory Planning**: The process of creating a path for a robot to follow over time.

**Tactile Sensing**: The ability to perceive touch, pressure, and texture through sensors.

**Torque Control**: Control of the rotational force applied by actuators.

## V

**VLA (Vision-Language-Action)**: A robotics paradigm that integrates visual perception, language understanding, and action execution.

**Vision System**: A system that enables a robot to perceive and interpret visual information.

**Virtual Reality (VR)**: A simulated experience that can be similar to or completely different from the real world.

**Voice Command**: A spoken instruction given to a robot or computer system.

## W

**Whole-Body Control**: A control approach that coordinates all parts of a robot simultaneously.

**Walking Pattern**: The sequence of foot placements and body movements in bipedal locomotion.

**Whisper**: OpenAI's automatic speech recognition system.

## Z

**ZMP (Zero Moment Point)**: A criterion for static balance in walking support, where the sum of moments of active forces is zero.

**ZMP Control**: A balance control method based on maintaining the ZMP within the support polygon.
